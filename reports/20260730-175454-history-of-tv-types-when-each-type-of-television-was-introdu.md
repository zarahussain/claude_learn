# A History of Television Display Technologies: From CRT to MicroLED

## Executive Summary

Television technology has undergone a series of radical transformations since the early 20th century. From the bulky cathode ray tubes (CRTs) that dominated living rooms for decades, through the flat-panel revolution of plasma and LCD displays, to today's self-emissive OLED and MicroLED panels, each new generation has delivered improvements in picture quality, energy efficiency, thinness, and price. This report traces the major display technologies used in consumer televisions, the approximate years they were introduced, and how each one works.

---

## 1. CRT — Cathode Ray Tube (1897 / Commercial TVs: 1934–early 2000s)

### How It Works
A CRT fires a beam of electrons from a cathode gun at a glass screen coated with phosphor. Magnetic coils steer the beam to "paint" each row of the image line by line at high speed. In colour sets, three separate electron guns target red, green, and blue phosphor dots arranged in a mask pattern across the screen.

### Key Milestones
- **1897** — German physicist Karl Ferdinand Braun invented the cathode ray tube. The first version capable of forming images was produced by John B. Johnson and Harry Weiner Weinhart of Western Electric, becoming a [commercial product in 1922](https://www.electronicdesign.com/technologies/industrial/displays/article/55126442/thomas-electronics-the-evolution-of-cathode-ray-tube-crt-monitor-technology).
- **1934** — The first commercially manufactured electronic televisions using CRTs were built by [Telefunken in Germany](https://www.electronicdesign.com/technologies/industrial/displays/article/55126442/thomas-electronics-the-evolution-of-cathode-ray-tube-crt-monitor-technology), with France, Britain, and the United States following shortly after.
- **1940s–1950s** — Established broadcast TV networks drove rapid mass adoption of CRT-based sets.
- **1954** — RCA introduced the first [colour CRT television sets](https://www.electronicdesign.com/technologies/industrial/displays/article/55126442/thomas-electronics-the-evolution-of-cathode-ray-tube-crt-monitor-technology) in the United States.
- **Late 1990s–2000s** — CRT production wound down as flat-panel alternatives took over. Worldwide LCD TV sales [surpassed CRT sales for the first time in Q4 2007](https://www.techwalla.com/articles/the-history-of-flat-screen-tvs).

### Legacy
CRTs defined the television experience for roughly 70 years. They offered excellent motion handling and colour accuracy for their era, but were heavy, deep, and power-hungry, and could not practically exceed ~40 inches diagonally.

## 2. Plasma Display Panel — PDP (Concept: 1964 / Consumer TV: 1997–2014)

### How It Works
A plasma display contains millions of tiny cells sandwiched between two glass panels. Each cell holds a mixture of noble gases (typically xenon and neon). An electrical charge excites the gas into a plasma state, causing it to emit ultraviolet light that then strikes a phosphor coating to produce visible red, green, or blue light. Each pixel is, in effect, a miniature fluorescent lamp and is self-emissive — it produces its own light without a backlight.

### Key Milestones
- **1964** — The concept originated at the [University of Illinois](https://spectrum.ieee.org/the-consumer-electronics-hall-of-fame-fujitsu-plasma-tv), where researchers Donald Bitzer and H. Gene Slottow devised the plasma display principle.
- **1973** — IBM produced the [first commercial plasma displays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_display_technology) with MgO protective coatings, used as banking terminal products.
- **1990–1995** — Fujitsu [commercialised colour plasma panels](https://spectrum.ieee.org/the-consumer-electronics-hall-of-fame-fujitsu-plasma-tv), first marketing a 21-inch PDP in 1993 and full-colour large-screen PDPs in 1995.
- **1997** — Fujitsu introduced [the first full-colour 42-inch consumer plasma TV](https://www.pocket-lint.com/tv/news/131549-farewell-plasma-tv-the-moments-that-defined-flatscreen/), retailing for more than $15,000.
- **Late 1999** — Plasma screens [entered the US consumer market](https://www.plasmatvbuyingguide.com/1-introductiontoplasmatv.html).
- **2006–2007** — LCD technology matured, and LCD TVs [took over the market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_display_technology) for sets 42 inches and under.
- **2014** — The last major plasma manufacturer (Panasonic) ceased production, [effectively ending the plasma era](https://www.pocket-lint.com/tv/news/131549-farewell-plasma-tv-the-moments-that-defined-flatscreen/).

### Strengths & Weaknesses
Plasma was praised for deep blacks, wide viewing angles, and smooth motion — advantages that rivalled and often surpassed early LCDs. Its drawbacks included higher power consumption, risk of image retention (burn-in), limited brightness in very large rooms, and difficulty achieving small screen sizes economically.

## 3. LCD — Liquid Crystal Display (First TV: 1982 / Mainstream: mid-2000s)

### How It Works
An LCD panel itself produces no light. Instead, a backlight (originally cold cathode fluorescent lamps, or CCFL tubes) illuminates the panel from behind. Liquid crystals — organic molecules that change their orientation when an electric field is applied — act as shutters in front of a colour filter matrix. By controlling the voltage at each pixel, the set determines how much of the backlight passes through and which colours are created. Early sets used CCFL backlights; this later evolved to LED backlighting (see Section 4).

### Key Milestones
- **1968** — RCA researchers first demonstrated a working [liquid crystal display](https://orientdisplay.com/knowledge-base/lcd-basics/lcd-history/).
- **1982** — Seiko Epson released the [Epson TV Watch](https://computer.fandom.com/wiki/LCD_television), the world's first LCD television — a tiny wristwatch-mounted active-matrix LCD display.
- **1983–1984** — Casio (TV-10) and Epson (ET-10) released [handheld pocket LCD televisions](https://computer.fandom.com/wiki/LCD_television).
- **1988** — Sharp introduced the [first commercial 14-inch large LCD TV](https://orientdisplay.com/knowledge-base/lcd-basics/lcd-history/) using active-matrix thin-film transistor (TFT) technology.
- **1997** — Sharp and Sony jointly unveiled a [42-inch PALC flat-screen TV](https://www.cannacompanionusa.com/insight/P0s/217/seeMSg7AU-when_did_flat-screens-come-out), priced above $15,000.
- **Mid-2000s** — Declining prices and improving image quality pushed LCDs into mainstream homes; by 2006, LCD TVs were [competitively sized and priced against plasma](https://www.techwalla.com/articles/the-history-of-flat-screen-tvs).
- **Q4 2007** — Global LCD TV unit sales [exceeded CRT TV sales for the first time](https://www.techwalla.com/articles/the-history-of-flat-screen-tvs).

### Strengths & Weaknesses
LCD panels are thinner, more power-efficient, and cheaper to manufacture than plasma, particularly at smaller sizes. The main weakness of CCFL-backlit LCDs was limited contrast ratio and uneven brightness — issues addressed by switching to LED backlighting.

---

## 4. LED-Backlit LCD (also marketed as "LED TV") (2006–present)

### How It Works
An LED TV is not a fundamentally different display type from LCD — it is still a liquid crystal panel. The distinction is the light source: instead of CCFL fluorescent tubes, arrays of light-emitting diodes (LEDs) illuminate the panel. Two main configurations exist:
- **Edge-lit LED**: LEDs run along the edges of the panel and a light guide distributes light across the screen. This enables very thin designs.
- **Full-array / direct-lit LED**: LEDs are arrayed across the entire back of the panel, allowing for **local dimming** — groups of LEDs can dim independently to improve contrast.

### Key Milestones
- **2004** — The [first mass-produced LED-backlit TV](https://shop.haierindia.com/blog/evolution-of-led-tv-technology/) is cited in some sources, though widespread commercial availability came later.
- **2007** — [Samsung introduced its first LED-LCD TV](https://shop.haierindia.com/blog/evolution-of-led-tv-technology/) to consumers.
- **2008** — Sony launched edge-lit LED technology in the 40-inch [BRAVIA KLV-40ZX1M](https://handwiki.org/wiki/Engineering:LED-backlit_LCD), and LG also released its first LED-LCD sets.
- **2009–2012** — LED backlighting [rapidly replaced CCFL](https://circuitcellar.com/research-design-hub/the-evolution-offlat-panel-tv-technology/) across virtually all LCD TV product lines due to its thinner form factor, lower power consumption, and superior local dimming potential.

### Strengths & Weaknesses
LED backlighting delivers higher brightness, richer colour, longer lamp life, and lower energy use than CCFL. Full-array local dimming greatly improved contrast ratio versus CCFL LCDs. However, LED-backlit LCDs still struggle to match the perfect blacks of self-emissive technologies like OLED, because the backlight cannot be fully eliminated at a per-pixel level.

## 5. OLED — Organic Light-Emitting Diode (First TV: 2008 / Mainstream: 2013–present)

### How It Works
OLED panels use a layer of organic carbon-based compounds that emit light when an electric current is passed through them. Each pixel is its own light source and can be switched off completely, producing absolute blacks and near-infinite contrast ratios. No backlight is required, enabling extremely thin and even flexible or rollable screen designs. Most consumer OLED TVs use a **White OLED + Colour Filter** (WOLED) architecture pioneered by LG, though Samsung later introduced a different approach (see QD-OLED, Section 7).

### Key Milestones
- **1987** — Kodak researchers Ching Wan Tang and Steven Van Slyke [demonstrated the first practical organic LED device](https://www.invent.org/inductees/steven-van-slyke).
- **2008** — Sony launched the [XEL-1](https://www.lg.com/uk/lg-experience/inspiration/the-history-of-the-oled-tv/), the world's first commercial OLED television — an 11-inch panel at a premium price.
- **2010** — LG introduced its [first OLED TV](https://www.displaymodule.com/blogs/knowledge/oled-history) for consumer purchase.
- **2012** — LG unveiled the [first 55-inch OLED TV](https://www.lg.com/uk/lg-experience/inspiration/the-history-of-the-oled-tv/), the largest OLED screen at the time. Samsung also showed a 55-inch OLED prototype at a price of approximately $9,000–$15,000.
- **2013** — LG [commercially launched its OLED TVs](https://www.lg.com/uk/lg-experience/inspiration/the-history-of-the-oled-tv/) to broader markets, gaining strong critical reception.
- **2014** — Samsung introduced [the first curved OLED TV](https://www.displaymodule.com/blogs/knowledge/oled-history).
- **2010s–2020s** — OLED became the gold standard for premium television picture quality, with LG as the dominant panel supplier.

### Strengths & Weaknesses
OLED offers perfect blacks, superior contrast, wide viewing angles, fast pixel response, and ultra-thin designs. Weaknesses include lower peak brightness compared to high-end LED LCDs, susceptibility to permanent image retention (burn-in) with static content over long periods, and historically higher cost than comparable LCD TVs.

---

## 6. QLED — Quantum Light-Emitting Diode (Quantum Dot LCD) (2017–present)

### How It Works
Despite the name, QLED is not a self-emissive technology — it is an **LED-backlit LCD** panel enhanced with a **quantum dot film**. Quantum dots are nanoscale semiconductor crystals that emit precisely tuned wavelengths of light when stimulated. The quantum dot layer sits between the LED backlight and the LCD panel, converting and narrowing the spectrum of the white backlight into purer red, green, and blue wavelengths. This substantially improves colour volume, colour accuracy, and peak brightness compared to standard LED LCDs. *Note: there is some debate and consumer confusion because the "QLED" marketing label implies an emissive LED display, but technically it remains an LCD-based technology.*

### Key Milestones
- **2013–2014** — Early quantum dot LCD products appeared in monitors and laptops.
- **2017** — [Samsung officially branded and launched its QLED TV line](https://advancematerialslab.com/qled-display-technology/), initially in 55-inch to 88-inch sizes.
- **2018–2020** — LG, Sony, TCL, Hisense, and other manufacturers [adopted quantum dot technology](https://nationalinterest.org/blog/techland/explainer-qled-vs-oled-vs-mini-led-tvs-whats-difference-177447) in their own product lines (under various brand names).

### Strengths & Weaknesses
QLED TVs can reach very high brightness levels (useful in well-lit rooms) and offer wide colour gamut at a competitive price point. They do not match OLED for black levels or contrast ratio since they still rely on an LCD shutter and backlight — a gap that Mini-LED backlighting has significantly narrowed.

---

## 7. Mini-LED (2019–present)

### How It Works
Mini-LED is an evolution of the LED backlight in a full-array LCD TV. Instead of a few hundred large LEDs in standard full-array sets, a Mini-LED TV uses **thousands of much smaller LEDs** (typically 100–200 micrometres in size) packed more densely across the back of the panel. This finer grid enables a far greater number of **local dimming zones** — sometimes thousands — allowing much more precise control over brightness and contrast, much closer to what OLED achieves. Mini-LED panels are commonly combined with quantum dot filters (branded as **Neo QLED** by Samsung, **QNED** by LG, etc.).

### Key Milestones
- **2019** — [TCL introduced the first Mini-LED consumer TVs](https://www.howtogeek.com/513194/what-is-a-mini-led-tv-and-why-would-you-want-one/) with its 8-Series models in October.
- **2020** — [TCL showcased Mini-LED as a challenge to OLED dominance at CES 2020](https://www.techradar.com/news/tcl-will-challenge-oled-dominance-at-ces-2020-with-its-new-mini-led-tvs).
- **2021** — [Samsung (Neo QLED) and LG (QNED)](https://www.tomsguide.com/news/year-of-the-mini-led-tv) launched their flagship Mini-LED TV lines, bringing the technology into the mainstream premium segment.
- **2021–present** — Mini-LED TVs have expanded across price tiers from multiple manufacturers.

### Strengths & Weaknesses
Mini-LED narrows the gap between LCD and OLED in contrast performance while offering significantly higher peak brightness than OLED. Residual light halo artefacts around bright objects on dark backgrounds ("blooming") remain a weakness compared to OLED's per-pixel dimming, though the issue improves with higher zone counts.

---

## 8. QD-OLED — Quantum Dot OLED (2022–present)

### How It Works
QD-OLED is a hybrid technology developed by Samsung Display that combines the best elements of OLED and quantum dot approaches. A blue OLED emitter layer provides the light, and **quantum dot colour converters** replace traditional colour filters to produce red and green sub-pixels. Because there is no white OLED + colour filter compromise (as in LG's WOLED), QD-OLED achieves higher colour volume, greater brightness, and richer colours than traditional OLED, while retaining OLED's self-emissive per-pixel control and perfect blacks.

### Key Milestones
- **January 2022** — Samsung unveiled its first QD-OLED televisions at [CES 2022](https://www.oled-info.com/samsung-electronics-unveil-its-first-qd-oled-tvs-ces-2022).
- **Early 2022** — Samsung launched the [S95B QD-OLED TV](https://www.etonline.com/samsungs-first-ever-qd-oled-tv-is-officially-here-experience-the-new-4k-oled-tv-and-2022-lineup) in 55-inch and 65-inch 4K configurations, with up to 1,500 nits peak brightness.
- **2023** — Samsung expanded to the [S90C and S95C models](https://www.techradar.com/televisions/samsungs-oled-tvs-shook-things-up-in-2023-heres-how-it-can-stay-on-top), adding a 77-inch size. Sony also adopted Samsung's QD-OLED panels in its own Bravia XR line.
- **2022–present** — QD-OLED has become [the premium rival to LG's WOLED panels](https://www.pocket-lint.com/oled-vs-qd-oled-vs-mini-led-vs-microled-different-tv-techs-explained/) in the high-end TV market.

## 9. MicroLED (Commercial Debut: 2018 / Consumer Sets: 2021–present)

### How It Works
MicroLED is considered the next frontier of display technology. It uses microscopic inorganic red, green, and blue LEDs — each smaller than 100 micrometres — as individual self-emissive pixels, eliminating both the LCD layer and any external backlight. Like OLED, each pixel can switch off completely for perfect blacks, but MicroLED uses inorganic materials, offering **higher brightness**, **longer lifespan**, and **no risk of burn-in** compared to organic OLED. The manufacturing challenge — placing millions of perfect microscopic LEDs onto a substrate with near-zero defect rates — has kept prices extremely high.

### Key Milestones
- **January 2018** — Samsung unveiled [**"The Wall"**](https://www.whathifi.com/news/samsungs-first-consumer-microled-tv-is-finally-arriving-in-early-2021), a 146-inch modular MicroLED display at CES 2018, marking the technology's commercial debut.
- **Q1 2021** — Samsung launched its first [consumer-ready, pre-fabricated 110-inch MicroLED TV](https://flatpanelshd.com/news.php?id=1607604514&subaction=showfull) for home use, though at an extremely high price.
- **2022–2025** — MicroLED TVs remained available only in very large sizes (89-inch and above) at costs in the tens of thousands of dollars, limiting them to luxury buyers.
- **2026** — Samsung and TCL are expanding their **Micro RGB / RGB Mini-LED** lineups — a technology that uses discrete RGB (red, green, blue) LEDs rather than white LEDs + colour filters, offering wider colour gamut and considered a step toward true MicroLED. [Industry analysts expect mainstream MicroLED to still be 3–5 years away](https://pixelight.fr/en/blog/en-microled-oled-vs-led-comparison-2026/) for typical consumers.

## Current Landscape and the Road Ahead (2026)

As of 2026, the television market segments broadly as follows:

- **Budget/Mid-range**: LED-backlit LCD (standard and QLED) dominates on price and brightness.
- **Premium**: OLED (LG WOLED) and QD-OLED (Samsung/Sony) compete for the title of best picture quality, with peak brightness now [exceeding 2,500 nits](https://electronics360.globalspec.com/article/23217/ces-2026-what-new-oled-micro-rgb-tvs-will-be-introduced) in flagship 2026 models.
- **High-performance LCD**: Mini-LED with quantum dots (Neo QLED, QNED, etc.) bridges the gap, offering OLED-rivalling contrast with higher brightness ceilings.
- **Cutting edge / luxury**: MicroLED and RGB Mini-LED (also called [Micro RGB or SQD Mini-LED](https://www.ecoustics.com/products/tcl-sqd-mini-led-tvs-2026/)) occupy the very top of the market at extreme price points.
- **Emerging**: True consumer-accessible MicroLED and [Micro-OLED for extended reality (XR) headsets](https://www.panoxdisplay.com/news/micro-oled-industry-report-2026-xr-display-trends.html) are the next frontiers.

AI-driven picture processing, voice integration (with assistants like Gemini and Copilot), and higher ambient-light-adaptive brightness management are the dominant non-panel trends shaping [2026 TV releases](https://www.consumerreports.org/electronics-computers/tvs/tv-trends-to-watch-for-a9692962731/).
